📖 Real Estate Commissions Post-NAR Settlement

The landmark August 2024 National Association of Realtors (NAR) settlement fundamentally restructured how residential commissions are negotiated in the United States:

  • Decoupling the 6% Commission: For decades, sellers routinely paid a blanket 5% to 6% commission that was split between the listing and buyer agents. Today, commission rates are completely unbundled and independently agreed upon.
  • Buyer Agent Agreements Required: Buyer agents must now execute written representation agreements with homebuyers before touring any properties, specifying exactly how much compensation they will receive.
  • Seller Concession Strategy: While sellers are not obligated to offer buyer agent compensation on the MLS, offering an unbundled 2% to 2.5% buyer concession frequently broadens the buyer pool, particularly for cash-constrained first-time buyers who cannot pay out-of-pocket agent fees.

References: National Association of Realtors (NAR) Settlement Fact Sheet; Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB) Buying a Home Guide.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

How did the 2024 NAR settlement change real estate commissions?

Under the landmark settlement, listing brokers are prohibited from offering buyer agent compensation on the MLS. Commission rates are now decoupled and independently negotiable by buyers and sellers.

Who pays the buyer agent commission now?

Buyers can pay their own agent directly, negotiate flat or hourly fees, or request a seller concession during purchase contract negotiations.

How does an agent-brokerage split work?

Real estate agents split their gross commissions with their sponsoring broker according to their agreement (e.g., 70/30 or 80/20), often minus a 5% to 6% national franchise royalty fee.

Is my home sale and mortgage balance kept confidential?

Yes. All property values, mortgage balances, and net proceeds run entirely in your local browser storage and are never uploaded or shared with real estate brokers or lenders.
